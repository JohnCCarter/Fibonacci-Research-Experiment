## Main Findings

### Short-span micro-bounce (watchlist)

`20171228T200000` is a micro-bounce with A→B span ~$1,100. At this span, fib levels
compress to nearly invisible hairlines in the zoom window — the artifact renders correctly
but is not practically reviewable at the current chart scale. The anchor placement itself
does not look suspicious; the issue is purely geometric.

This is an isolated instance in the sample. `20171219T160000` (~$2,200 span) renders
cleanly — the threshold is span-size-dependent, not a sene-2017 systemic issue.

**Approximate span threshold observed:** below ~$1,500, levels are unreadable in the
current zoom window; above ~$2,000, fine.

### Body/close vs wick convention (observation, not error)

Two fibs share anchor_b at ~$30,500: `20210110T080000` and `20210110T200000`. Both
anchor at the Jan 10 body/close level, not the wick extreme (~$28,500). The ~$2,000
gap between body and wick is significant, but the **consistency between the two fibs**
(separate labels that independently arrived at the same anchor_b) indicates a deliberate
methodological choice, not a mistake.

`20210225T120000` (Feb crash) likely uses the same body/close convention for its
anchor_b, but harder to isolate without a direct pair comparison.

**Status:** observed pattern, not a documented global labeling rule yet. Watchlist for
documentation; no label changes until verified in the labeling tool.

### Sub-leg structure is correct

All reviewed fibs capture specific structural sub-legs (e.g. "first breakdown leg Jan 10
20:00 → Jan 11 low"), not full ATH-to-bottom sequences. Each fib has a well-defined A→B
with clear structural justification. The high density in `2021_dec2020_mar2021` reflects
many distinct sub-legs in the Jan–Mar 2021 period — each labeled separately. This is
correct 4H methodology.

### Zoom resolves Tier 1 readability

In every OK/OK-with-note sample, the Tier 2 zoom chart resolved what Tier 1 could not
show: individual anchors, level spacing, direction of the sub-leg. The Tier 1 limitation
was purely visual density, not a data or labeling quality problem.

### 2021 vs 2017_h2

2021 samples have longer spans ($8k–$12k) and consistently readable levels. 2017_h2 has
more variable spans; short micro-bounces in the parabolens slutfas are more common. No
2021-specific structural problems found.

## Conclusion

No suspicious fibs found in this sample. The Tier 2 zoom artifacts function as intended —
per-fib windowed charts resolve the Tier 1 map readability problem for both scopes.

The two watchlist items are documentation/precision gaps, not labeling errors:

1. Some micro-bounce fibs have spans too short for levels to be visually useful in the
   zoom window — geometric limitation, not a structural error.
2. Body/close vs wick appears to be a consistent local convention in crash-leg anchors —
   not yet in the labeling docs.

**No source labels should be changed based on this sample-pass.** Any future changes
require explicit review in the labeling tool with the specific fib open.
